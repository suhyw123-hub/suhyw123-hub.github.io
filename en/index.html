The Name "Hagah Dabar"

הָגָה · דָּבָר

'Hagah' is a Hebrew word meaning 'to utter aloud, to murmur or muse quietly.' In Psalm 1, the word for 'meditate' in 'on his law he meditates day and night' is this exact word. The same word is used for a lion growling low over its prey (Isa 31:4) and a dove cooing (Isa 38:14).

Meditation in the Bible is not merely mental thought. It is bringing the Word to our lips, repeating it over and over, and engraving it upon our hearts.

'Dabar' means 'word.' It refers both to what God has spoken and to the fulfillment of that word.

Therefore, Hagah Dabar means 'to murmur the Word.' We created this app so that even on busy days, you can bring a verse of Scripture to your lips and, when you have time, delve deeply into His Word.

Phone and PC, One Meditation

The two apps work seamlessly together. Continue your meditation, started on your phone while on the go, deeply at home on your PC.

On Your Phone While on the Go

Read today's verse and a portion of your reading plan during your commute or break, and leave your thoughts in a short meditation note. Continue watching sermon, prayer, and praise videos right where you left off.

Deeply on Your PC at Home

On a wide screen, place verse connections, the flow of the Word, commentaries, originals, and classic books side by side, and expand the notes left on your phone into research notes.

⇄ Connected on the Same Wi-Fi

Notes, favorites, and reading plan progress from your phone sync to your PC, and materials organized on your PC go to your phone. They communicate directly over your home Wi-Fi without uploading to an internet server.

Key Features

Easy Commentary by Verse

Tap a verse to open commentary, meditation questions, and prayer. You can also view the flow of chapters and paragraphs in one place.

Cross-References by Meaning

Discover how each verse connects across the entire Bible and why, complete with explanations. Walk through the Scriptures from Genesis to Revelation following the flow of the Word.

Commentaries & Original Languages

Read classic commentaries in contemporary language and trace where Hebrew and Greek words appear throughout the Bible.

Meditation Plans & Lens Meditation

Set your daily portion from Bible reading to thematic meditation, and explore a single theme from multiple angles.

Classic Books & Sermons

Read Christian classics by Spurgeon, Murray, Bounds, and others translated into modern language.

Sermon, Prayer & Hymn Videos

Listen to related sermons, prayers, and hymns by verse and language, and pick up right where you left off.

AI Assistant

Ask your chosen AI about a verse, compile multiple answers into a single note, and save it to your research notebook.
